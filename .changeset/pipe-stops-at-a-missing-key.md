**breaking** feat: a `pipe` that starts with an `optional` runs no step for a missing key and leaves the key out, as an entry of `object`, `strip_object`, or `loose_object`: `object({a: pipe(optional(string), check(...))})` failed on `{}` when the check refused `undefined` and now accepts it, as in Zod and Valibot, and `object({a: pipe(optional(string), transform(...))})` gave `{a: ...}` for `{}` and now gives `{}`, as in Valibot and not in Zod, so write `with_default` to fill a missing key in; with the key there, as `undefined` or inherited too, every step runs as before, and so it does outside an object, at the top level, as an array's item, and as a record's value; a pipe starts with an `optional` by that schema's kind, so under `message`, `flavor`, `brand`, and `meta` too, and through every pipe it starts with, and it runs no step under a `nullable`, a `lazy`, or a `union` either, which hand it the object and the key; after a `nullable`, a `lazy`, or a `union` that reaches an `optional`, a `with_default`, and a `check` or a `transform`, the steps run as before; such a pipe has a `run` of four parameters, chosen when it is made; the Rust crate is unchanged, as a pipe that starts with an `optional` can't be written to the interchange form
